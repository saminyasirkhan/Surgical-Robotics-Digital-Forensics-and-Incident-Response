# 🔍 Surgical Robotics Digital Forensics & Incident Response

This project examines the **digital forensic challenges associated with surgical robotic systems** in healthcare environments, focusing on the preservation, acquisition and integrity of digital evidence during incident investigations.

It compares the technical architectures of the **da Vinci Xi** and **Versius** surgical robotic systems and develops a draft **Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)** to guide forensic investigations involving NHS surgical robots.

## 📄 Project Overview

The project investigates how proprietary robotic architectures, network connectivity, local storage and cloud-based services influence digital forensic investigations.

It proposes a structured incident response and evidence-handling procedure that balances forensic integrity, legal requirements and patient safety.

**Key areas explored:**
- Digital forensics in healthcare and surgical robotic systems
- Embedded operating systems and forensic evidence sources
- Network connectivity and cloud-based telemetry
- Volatile and non-volatile evidence preservation
- Digital evidence identification and acquisition
- Incident response and forensic investigation procedures
- Chain of custody and evidential integrity
- Patient safety, clinical continuity and data protection

## 🔍 Key Components

### 1. Surgical Robotic System Analysis
- Examined the architecture and operational characteristics of the da Vinci Xi and Versius surgical robotic systems.
- Compared embedded operating systems, storage mechanisms and network connectivity.
- Investigated diagnostic logs, surgical telemetry, video recordings and cloud-based evidence sources.
- Evaluated the forensic implications of proprietary architectures and manufacturer-managed systems.

### 2. Digital Forensic Evidence & Acquisition Challenges
- Identified potential forensic evidence across local storage, system logs, removable media and cloud infrastructure.
- Examined the risks of volatile evidence loss during shutdowns and power-state changes.
- Considered evidence accessibility limitations resulting from proprietary software and manufacturer restrictions.
- Evaluated the importance of coordinating forensic acquisition with NHS clinical and technical personnel.

### 3. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Development
- Developed a draft SOP for investigating incidents involving NHS surgical robotic systems.
- Defined responsibilities for police investigators, digital forensic examiners, NHS personnel and manufacturer engineers.
- Established procedures for initial scene assessment, evidence preservation and digital evidence acquisition.
- Incorporated clinical safety and operational continuity requirements into the investigative process.

### 4. Evidence Integrity & Chain of Custody
- Applied the ACPO digital evidence principles to support forensic integrity.
- Considered evidence documentation, secure handling and chain-of-custody requirements.
- Examined the importance of maintaining reliable and auditable forensic records.
- Addressed the risks associated with unauthorised system modifications and evidence contamination.

### 5. Legal, Regulatory & Operational Considerations
- Considered the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE) when defining lawful investigative authority.
- Examined UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 requirements relating to patient information.
- Evaluated the challenges of conducting forensic investigations in active healthcare environments.
- Prioritised patient safety and clinical continuity throughout the proposed investigative procedures.

## 📎 Project Notes

This project presents a research-based digital forensic investigation framework for surgical robotic systems operating within NHS healthcare environments.

The SOP is a proposed investigative procedure developed through technical research and analysis, rather than a validated operational procedure or an investigation conducted on physical surgical robotic equipment.
